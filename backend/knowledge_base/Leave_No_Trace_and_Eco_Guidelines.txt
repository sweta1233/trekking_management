SECTION 1: THE VULNERABILITY OF FRAGILE ALPINE ECOSYSTEMS
High-altitude alpine meadows (Bugyals) and glacial river basins possess fragile topsoil and slow-growing vegetation adapted to extreme ultraviolet exposure and sub-zero winters. Irresponsible tourism and plastic accumulation degrade these delicate natural sanctuaries.

SECTION 2: THE SEVEN LEAVE NO TRACE (LNT) PRINCIPLES
1. Plan Ahead and Prepare: Understand local regulations, weather patterns, and permit requirements before entering national parks or biosphere reserves.
2. Travel and Camp on Durable Surfaces: Stick strictly to established trails to prevent multiple treadways and meadow erosion. Pitch tents on designated rock or bare ground camps, never directly on fragile flowering alpine turf.
3. Dispose of Waste Properly:
- Pack It In, Pack It Out: Every non-biodegradable item (energy gel packets, wet wipes, candy wrappers, tetra packs) must be carried back to municipal collection centers.
- Organic Waste: Biodegradable food scraps decay exceedingly slowly at freezing temperatures and attract wildlife. Scraps must be composted in designated deep pits or packed out.
4. Leave What You Find: Preserve cultural artifacts, cairns, prayer stones (Mani stones), alpine fossils, and rare flora like Brahma Kamal.
5. Minimize Campfire Impacts: Campfires are strictly prohibited inside biosphere reserves and above the tree line (3,200m) due to acute shortage of dead wood.
6. Respect Wildlife: Observe Himalayan monal, musk deer, snow leopards, and blue sheep from a safe distance. Store food securely in bear/rodent-proof rucksack bags.
7. Be Considerate of Other Trekkers: Maintain quiet in campsites after 20:00. Yield trail right-of-way to ascending hikers and pack animals (mules/yaks) on the cliff side (never the valley side).

SECTION 3: HUMAN WASTE SANITATION AT ALPINE CAMPS
- At designated campsites, dry eco-toilet tents equipped with sawdust, ash, and organic composting pits are mandatory.
- When trekking in open wilderness without toilet facilities, dig a 'cathole' 6 to 8 inches deep, located at least 60 to 70 meters (200 feet) away from streams, glacial springs, and trails.
- All toilet paper, wipes, and feminine hygiene products must be sealed in odor-proof ziplock bags and carried out. Never bury synthetic wipes.
